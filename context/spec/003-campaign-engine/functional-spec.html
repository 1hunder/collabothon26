Finance Quest Campaign Engine

Topic: The playable Finance Quest campaign: creating a hero, investigating a story turn by turn with dice checks, locations and CommerzBuddy, committing an investment decision, and seeing the real outcome, debrief and XP  ·  Author: Viktor Kulyk (drafted with Claude)
In Review Verification: 0 of 110 criteria verified

At a glance

  • Finance Quest's real game: a short role-play adventure built around one anonymized company's real history.
  • New players create a hero and pick a class — the Guardian (more HP), the Analyst (one reroll) or the Investigator (one roll with advantage).
  • Turn by turn the player types actions, travels between Market Square, the Library and generated campaign locations, rolls dice for uncertain investigation steps and can summon CommerzBuddy any time; every turn is remembered in a journal.
  • When ready, the player locks a decision with evidence, a probability forecast and confidence; only then is the real outcome revealed and HP updated from real prices.
  • A debrief with a 'What if…' panel, a reflection, the company reveal and up to 100 XP for reasoning close the quest; narration is read aloud by default.

Requirements

2.1 First entry: create your heroInterview

New players create a named hero with a class; returning players are greeted.

When the player taps "Start" on a Finance Quest card and no hero exists on this phone and browser, the game first shows "Create your hero": 1. A name field, pre-filled with "Lena". The name is trimmed of leading and trailing spaces and must be 2 to 20 characters long. Letters, numbers, spaces, hyphens and apostrophes are allowed, and the field accepts no more than 20 characters. 2. The three class cards (2.2). The player selects one. 3. A "Begin" button, enabled only when the name is valid and a class is selected.

After "Begin", the game shows "What are you into?" (2.3).

When a hero already exists, "Start" skips hero creation, greets the hero by name ("Welcome back, [name]") and opens "What are you into?".

When a quest is in progress, the Finance Quest card shows "Continue" instead of "Start" (2.15).

The hero is kept on this phone and browser. Name, class and total XP carry over to the next campaign. HP starts fresh at the class's starting value every campaign. In this version a hero cannot be renamed, changed to another class or deleted.

Hero creation comes before "What are you into?" for new players only. This changes spec 001, which said Start opens "What are you into?" directly; spec 001 needs a matching amendment. The persistent hero is a deliberate exception to the product definition's "no multi-mission progression". There are no leaderboards or class trees.

2.4 Scenes and turnsInterview Web

Turns: free text or suggestions, DM replies, decision offered when everything reachable is known.

The scene screen: from top to bottom it shows:

the hero status bar (2.11)

the location bar (2.6)

the current location's background art

the scene log: the DM's narration and earlier turns, scrollable

the question "What would you do?"

a text box with a send button

up to 4 suggestion buttons:

the CommerzBuddy, journal and voice buttons

Acting:

The player answers by typing a free-form action or tapping a suggested action. A suggested action fills the text box, and the player sends it.

The send button is disabled while the text box is empty or contains only spaces.

How the game handles an action: it decides which kind of action it is.

Free action: anything that doesn't attempt one of the campaign's investigation challenges. Examples: reading information, asking for a definition, moving to another location, talking to a character. It succeeds without dice.

Challenge action: an action that attempts one of the investigation challenges the DM has set up. It triggers that challenge's dice check (2.5).

Decision intent: the "I'm ready to decide" button, or a typed message such as "I want to decide" or "I'm ready to invest". A typed message makes the DM ask "Ready to make your decision?" with the button.

The DM's reply:

"The Dungeon Master is writing…" appears within 1 second of sending.

The reply then appears word by word as it arrives.

Each reply is at most 150 words.

Each reply ends either with "What would you do?" or with a question offering the next options.

Off-topic or unclear actions: the DM answers briefly and steers back to the investigation. There is no dice check, and the action still counts as a turn.

When a reply doesn't arrive: if no reply has started within 15 seconds, or the reply fails, the message "The Dungeon Master lost the thread." appears with a "Try again" button. The player's action is kept and is sent again when they tap it.

Information the player needs is never hidden behind luck. The chart, definitions and every key evidence clue are always reachable without a dice check.

Offering the decision: the "I'm ready to decide" button appears once both of these are true:

the DM has finished describing the scene and the situation

the player has been shown every clue that can be reached at that moment

At that point the DM says there is nothing more to uncover for now. The player can still keep investigating, or ask to decide by typing at any time.

2.5 Dice checksInterview Web

Deterministic d20 checks with fail-forward and class powers.

Challenges and difficulties:

The DM sets up investigation challenges around clues from the reference document. Each challenge's difficulty is taken from this fixed rule table according to its kind, never chosen by the narrator. Each challenge gives one clue on success:

DifficultyKind of challengeClue it gives on success
8finding a public company reporta basic clue
12spotting an unusual financial figurean extra insight
15cross-checking conflicting evidencea stronger insight
18uncovering an obscure leada bonus clue or shortcut

Every campaign has at least 2 challenges on the way to the key evidence, so a player who finds all key evidence meets at least 2 dice checks. A campaign has at most 4 challenges.

Each challenge is rolled once per campaign. Attempting it again after a setback gets a DM reminder that the information must come from elsewhere, and no new roll.

The dice check card: when the player attempts a challenge, the card shows:

the challenge

the difficulty

the hero's bonus (+2)

a "Roll" button

for an Investigator with Keen Eye left, a second button "Keen Eye — roll with advantage (1 left)", which rolls two dice and uses the higher

Rolling:

Tapping "Roll" animates a 20-sided die for about 1 second. The card then shows the die result, the bonus, the total, and "Success" or "Setback":

On success the DM narrates how the player finds the challenge's clue.

On a setback the DM narrates a complication and names another source, conversation or location where the same essential information can be found. The essential information never becomes unreachable.

Order of events after a setback:

1. The card shows "Setback". 2. An Analyst with a reroll left also sees "Second Look — reroll (1 left)" and "Accept setback":

3. Any other hero sees only "Continue". 4. The DM narrates once the result is final. The DM never narrates the first result if the player rerolled.

Market Square, Library and generated campaign locations.

The location bar:

Always visible at the top of every scene, under the hero status bar.

Lists every location, with the current one highlighted.

Tapping another location moves there. A move is a free action, counts as a turn, and makes the DM describe the new place.

The bar is disabled while a dice check card is open.

Fixed locations, present in every campaign:

Market Square: market rumours, other characters, and the in-period news event (2.9).

Library: a research terminal, like a Bloomberg terminal. Here the player can open, without dice:

Campaign locations: 1 to 3 locations unique to the campaign. The DM generates them from the reference document when the campaign starts. Examples: "the Factory", "Headquarters", "the Trade Fair".

Art:

Fixed locations use the team's prepared backgrounds. Until those are delivered, a neutral placeholder background with the location name is shown.

Campaign locations get generated artwork, created when the campaign starts. It always follows the game's art style guide (palette, lighting, viewpoint, level of detail), so it matches the fixed locations and other campaigns.

Until a location's artwork is ready, its placeholder background with the location name is shown, and the player can play there meanwhile. The artwork replaces the placeholder as soon as it is ready.

No location art shows real logos, brand names or recognisable real people.

2.7 CommerzBuddy on callInterview Codebase

Summon CommerzBuddy anytime for answers, never advice.

Summoning Buddy:

A CommerzBuddy button is visible on every Finance Quest screen except "Create your hero" and "What are you into?".

Tapping it brings Buddy on screen with an appearance animation (a placeholder until the team's animation arrives). It opens a panel where the player can ask any question.

What Buddy does:

Answers in plain language, in at most 120 words.

Explains concepts, for example "What is a drawdown?".

Helps the player reflect.

Before the decision is locked, uses only information up to story today.

What Buddy never does:

tells the player what to buy, sell or do

promises a profit

reveals what happens next before the reveal

names the real company before the company reveal (2.14)

Behaviour:

Asked "Should I invest?", Buddy turns it back into a reasoning question, for example "That's your call — what evidence would make you more or less confident?"

Talking to Buddy doesn't use a turn or a dice check. Every question and answer is saved to the journal.

After the reveal, Buddy can explain the result. After the company reveal, it can talk about the real company.

Closing the panel returns the player to the same screen, with whatever was typed still in the text box.

Acceptance criteria — 6 (0 verified)
  • Button visibleWhen the player is on any scene, the commit screen, the reveal, the debrief or the XP screen, then the CommerzBuddy button is visible.
  • SummonWhen the player taps the CommerzBuddy button, then Buddy appears with an animation and a panel opens with a text box.
  • DefinitionWhen the player asks "What is a drawdown?", then Buddy answers in plain language in no more than 120 words.
  • No buy/sellWhen the player asks "Should I buy this?", then Buddy does not say buy or sell and asks about the player's evidence or confidence.
  • No futureWhen the player asks before locking what happens to the price next, then Buddy says it can't know the future and suggests what evidence to look at.

2.10 Voice narrationInterview Web

Narration read aloud by default with a remembered toggle.

What is read aloud: the DM's narration, Buddy's answers, the reveal summary and the debrief. Voice is on by default.

Starting the voice:

Phones only allow sound after a tap, so voice starts with the tap that opens the campaign ("Find my quest") or resumes it ("Continue").

After that, each new reply is read automatically.

The speaker button:

It appears on every Finance Quest screen and turns voice on or off.

The choice is remembered on the same phone and browser.

Turning voice on reads the next reply, not one already shown.

Stopping:

Tapping the speaker button while text is being read stops reading at once and turns voice off.

Sending a new action, or opening the Buddy panel, stops any reading still in progress.

Voices and text:

Buddy is read in a different voice from the DM.

All text is always shown on screen too.

2.13 Reveal and HP updateInterview

Time skip and reveal with real portfolio math and HP.

The time skip: after locking, a short time-skip screen ("[horizon] later…") moves story today to the end of the horizon.

The reveal: it shows the real, still-anonymized outcome:

"Locked at [time] · horizon [horizon]"

the price chart, now extended through the horizon

the portfolio breakdown:

the HP change, for example "100 HP → 90 HP"

How the numbers are calculated:

The investment's return is the change from the closing price on story today to the closing price on the last day of the horizon.

Spread uses half the company's return and half the fund's return.

Euro amounts are shown to the cent. Percentages are rounded to one decimal place.

HP = final value ÷ €10, rounded to the nearest whole number.

Example: €400 invested, €600 kept, investment −25% → €600 + €300 = €900 → 90 HP.

Every number on the reveal comes from the reference document, not from the narrator.

Acceptance criteria — 5 (0 verified)
  • Time skipWhen the decision is locked, then a "[horizon] later…" screen appears, followed by the reveal with the extended chart.
  • Worked exampleWhen a hero with €1,000 invested €400, kept €600, and the investment fell 25%, then the reveal shows a final value of €900.00, a loss of €100.00 (−10.0%), and "100 HP → 90 HP".
  • Don't investWhen the player chose "Don't invest" or "Wait", then the reveal shows a final value equal to the starting budget and HP unchanged.
  • Same %When a Guardian and an Analyst invest the same share of their budget in the same campaign, then the reveal shows the same percentage result for both.
  • Lock stampWhen the reveal is shown, then it shows "Locked at [time] · horizon [horizon]".

2.14 Debrief, reflection, company reveal and XPInterview

Debrief with What if…, reflection, company reveal, XP, finish.

The screens follow in this order: reveal → debrief (with "What if…") → reflection → company reveal → XP → Finish.

Debrief: the DM's short debrief has three labelled parts:

Sound reasoning: what was reasonable in the player's own words.

Overlooked: the names of clues from the reference document the player didn't find, or "Nothing important" if they found them all.

Luck: how much of the result depended on events no one could know at story today.

It ends with one forecast line, for example "You said 70% likely higher — it ended lower."

"What if…" (rewind): the debrief ends with a "What if…" panel. It shows how the same starting budget would have ended, using the same real prices, under each of:

Don't invest

Invest half

Invest everything

Spread everything

The player's own choice is highlighted. The panel never changes the player's HP or XP.

Reflection:

The debrief asks one question, for example "What would you check first next time?".

An answer of at least 10 characters is required.

"Submit reflection" stays disabled until the answer is long enough.

Company reveal: after the reflection is submitted, the game shows the real company name, the real dates and a short source note.

2.16 SafeguardsInterview Codebase

No future info, no advice, no invented numbers.

Nothing gives away the outcome before the decision is locked. No DM text, Buddy answer, chart, card or location art shows:

a price after story today

any identifying detail

No investment advice: no DM or Buddy text recommends buying or selling a security or promises a profit. No separate disclaimer line is shown in this demo.

No invented numbers:

Numbers that must come from the reference document: prices, returns, percentages, euro amounts and financial figures about the company.

Numbers the narrator may use freely: dice values, difficulties, HP, turn counts, the horizon and general examples in definitions.

If a DM or Buddy reply contains a company figure that isn't in the reference document, the player never sees it. The reply is replaced with one that leaves the figure out, or, if that fails, with "Let's check the numbers in the Library."

3. Scope and Boundaries

In-Scope

  • Hero creation on first entry, and a persistent hero (name, class, total XP, learning history) on the same phone and browser
  • Three classes: the Guardian (+20 HP), the Analyst (one reroll per campaign) and the Investigator (one roll with advantage per campaign)
  • "What are you into?" with six interests, starting a campaign built around a reference document (a sample one until the reference-document spec exists)
  • Adventure loop: scenes, free-text actions with suggestions, DM narration, free and challenge actions, off-topic handling, "Try again" on a failed reply
  • Dice checks for 2–4 investigation challenges with difficulties from the fixed rule table, natural 20 and natural 1, fail-forward, the Analyst reroll and the Investigator's advantage
  • Locations: Market Square and Library (fixed), 1–3 campaign locations generated with the campaign and given generated art that follows one style guide, and placeholders until art is ready
  • CommerzBuddy on call
  • Journal and memory of every turn
  • The in-period news event
  • Voice narration, on by default, with an on/off toggle that is remembered
  • Hero status bar and hero screen with learning history
  • Commit with decision, amount, evidence, forecast and confidence; lock with confirmation
  • Time skip, reveal with portfolio math and HP, debrief with "What if…", reflection, company reveal, XP, Finish
  • Focus (3 per campaign) with two optional actions, and three earnable items
  • Leaving and continuing exactly where the player left off
  • The safeguards in 2.16

Out-of-Scope

  • Producing reference documents: finding a real company and period for the player's interests, gathering its data, anonymizing it and testing re-identification. This is a separate specification; this spec uses a sample reference document until it exists.
  • Final art and animation: the backgrounds for Market Square and the Library, and CommerzBuddy's appearance animation. The team will deliver these; placeholders are used meanwhile.
  • The €25 StartDepot savings-plan step after the quest. This is the separate end-of-quest spec; it is optional and never affects XP.
  • The stage presentation page.
  • Hero changes: renaming, changing class, or deleting a hero.
  • Other game-loop items: the Planner (Cleric) class, gold or a treasure economy, spells, branching multi-campaign storylines, and multiplayer.
  • Real login, accounts across devices, or saving the hero anywhere other than the player's phone and browser.
  • Languages other than English. Voice is English only.
